Talk to your hardware from your phone.

NexTerm is a USB serial terminal for Android. Plug in an adapter or a development board with an OTG cable, read what it prints, send commands and watch the control lines.

Download for Android First connection
  • Free and open source
  • No Internet permission
  • Android 8.0 and newer
  • 5 driversCH340, CP210x, FTDI, PL2303 and CDC-ACM
  • 3 viewsConsole, Trace and Hex over the same data
  • 300 to 1,500,000baud, or any value you type
  • 50,000 linesof scrollback, at most

One stream, three views

Read it the way the problem needs.

All three views are drawn from the same record of the session, so switching never loses a byte.

Adapter details

Know exactly what you plugged in.

Tap any adapter in the list and its whole identity unfolds, before you even connect.

  • Who it says it is. Manufacturer, product and serial number, as the chip reports them.
  • Where it is. The VID:PID of its model and the path Android gave it.
  • How NexTerm talks to it. The driver in use, and whether it was detected, added by NexTerm or chosen by you.
  • What it can do. The control lines and the flow control modes that driver supports.
  • Where it stands. Whether the USB permission is granted, and the profile it will connect with.

A chip NexTerm does not recognise? Choose its driver yourself, once, for every profile of that model.

Saved commands

Your macros, one tap away.

Keep the commands you type again and again. Tap one to send it, tap the pencil to change it.

  • Text or raw bytes. Give each one a name and say whether it is text or hex bytes.
  • Its own line ending. The session default, CR, LF, CR+LF or none, command by command.
  • Always at hand. One list for every profile, open from the Console, Trace and Hex.
you write 01 03 "OK" 0D 0A
it sends 01 03 4F 4B 0D 0A

Full screen

Give the terminal the whole screen.

One round button hides the header and the bars: all the lines, none of the chrome.

  • Drag it out of the way. The button goes anywhere over the terminal, and NexTerm remembers its place for each orientation.
  • The same button brings it all back. Nothing to learn, no gesture to remember.
  • Fit more in. Pinch to zoom from 7 to 24 sp, and the screen stays on during a session.

Settings

Tune it to the way you work.

A few short sections: a list of pages on a phone, two columns on a wide screen.

  • Terminal. Text size from 7 to 24 sp with a live sample, Local echo, Autoscroll, Scrollback from 1,000 to 50,000 lines, and the extra keys you want in the row.
  • Connection. Auto-reconnect, going straight to the terminal after Connect, and the state of DTR and RTS when the port opens.
  • App. Show or hide the bar of the control lines and the names under the icons.

And the rest of the toolbox

Small things that save the day at the bench.

Control lines in reach

Toggle DTR and RTS, watch CTS, DSR, CD and RI, send a BREAK. Choose their state when the port opens: some boards reset when it changes.

DTRRTSCTSDSRCDRI

Profiles that remember

Baud rate, data bits, parity, stop bits, flow control and line ending, saved for each adapter. Connect with one tap, and let Auto-reconnect resume the session after an unplug.

The session at a glance

How long it has lasted, the bytes received and sent, the speed, the errors, and a chart of the last minute of traffic.

A layout for each screen

A bottom bar on a phone, a rail and a dock of commands on a tablet, in portrait or landscape.

Made for fingers

A row of extra keys for ESC, TAB, CTRL and the arrows, with Ctrl and Alt that stay down for one key.

It keeps running

An open session stays alive while you use another app, with a notification that lets you disconnect.

Adapters and boards

The chips you already have in the drawer.

NexTerm picks the driver from the VID:PID of the adapter. Boards with a USB port of their own work too, as long as they show up as a serial port.

CH340 / CH341Cheap adapters and many clone boards
CP2102 / CP2104Silicon Labs CP210x family
FTDIFT232R, FT2232 and FT231X, with several ports
PL2303Prolific adapters
CDC-ACMNative USB of the ESP32-S2 and S3, Arduino and STM32

Open and private

Yours to inspect, and nobody else's to read.

Free software

NexTerm is released under the GNU General Public License v3. Read the code, build it yourself, report a problem or send a fix on GitHub.

Private by design

The app does not ask for the Internet permission, so it cannot send anything off your device. Your profiles, commands and settings stay on it.

Get NexTerm

Download the APK from GitHub and open it on your phone or tablet. It needs Android 8.0 or newer, USB host support and an OTG cable.
